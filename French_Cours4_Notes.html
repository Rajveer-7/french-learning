2 · Numbers 20–69

Tens pattern: hyphenate for most digits, but use "et" only for x1 (21, 31, 41, 51...). Tap a row to hear it.

Pattern: vingt-deux, trente-trois, quarante-cinq… but vingt et un, trente et un, quarante et un, cinquante et un. The teacher confirmed: "we do not use et with other numbers."

3 · Numbers 70–100 (bonus — beyond today's class)

Not covered in class yet, but added here so you can count all the way to 100. France French reuses the 60s and 80s: 70 = "soixante-dix" (60+10), 80 = "quatre-vingts" (4×20), 90 = "quatre-vingt-dix" (4×20+10).

4 · The counting game

From the textbook (Saison 1, p.21) — a fun drill for counting fluently under pressure.

The teacher throws an imaginary ball to a student while starting to count. That student receives it and throws it on while continuing to count. A second imaginary ball is added, then a third, all at once. First to reach 69 without a mistake wins!
