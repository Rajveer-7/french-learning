Pourquoi ça marche comme çaPourquoi « il » ne veut rien dire dans « il pleut »

Nobody is raining. The il in il pleut and il fait froid refers to nothing at all — it is an empty placeholder, exactly like the "it" in English "it's raining".

French requires every sentence to have a subject, so when there is no real one, it supplies a dummy. That is also why the il never becomes elle, and why you never see fait chaud standing alone.

Same mechanism in il y a and il faut — two more phrases where il is holding a seat for nobody.
