Pourquoi ça marche comme çaPourquoi le français a deux mots que l'anglais n'a pas

English can answer “Do you want coffee?” with just “Yes, I do.” — the noun simply vanishes and nothing marks the hole it left.

French will not allow that. Every grammatical slot must keep a trace, so when the noun goes, a pronoun takes its place. EN and Y are the two pronouns that cover the slots after de and à — the two slots English leaves empty.

That is also why they feel so foreign and why they are so frequent: they are filling gaps your native language taught you to ignore.

Pourquoi ça marche comme çaPourquoi « il » ne veut rien dire dans « il pleut »

Nobody is raining. The il in il pleut and il fait froid refers to nothing at all — it is an empty placeholder, exactly like the "it" in English "it's raining".

French requires every sentence to have a subject, so when there is no real one, it supplies a dummy. That is also why the il never becomes elle, and why you never see fait chaud standing alone.

Same mechanism in il y a and il faut — two more phrases where il is holding a seat for nobody.
