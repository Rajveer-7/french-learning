Pourquoi ça marche comme çaPourquoi on « a » faim au lieu d'« être » faim

English treats hunger as a state you are in. French treats it as a thing you possess: j'ai faim, literally "I have hunger".

Neither is more logical — they are two different metaphors for the same feeling. But the French one is older: Latin did the same thing.

This matters beyond A1. At B2 and C1, formal French keeps reaching for nouns where English reaches for verbs — la mise en œuvre du projet rather than "implementing the project". J'ai faim is where that habit starts.
